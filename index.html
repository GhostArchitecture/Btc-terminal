Method — how every number is made

Tape and index. The plotted price is a sticky-leader tape: one exchange book plots at a time — the leader, chosen by latency priority — streaming its prints at full socket speed, while the other feeds run verification. Every print must be confirmed by a CF-constituent peer within a dynamic tolerance (three times the live dispersion of the CF-constituent books excluding the print itself, floored at 6 bp, capped at 15 bp); a leader that goes silent for 3 s or prints an unconfirmed outlier is demoted and the next fresh confirmed book takes the line. Index sources never plot. Separately, a settlement index proxy — the median of the fresh CF Benchmarks constituent books this instrument holds (Coinbase, Kraken, Bitstamp, Gemini) — is computed each second, because Kalshi settles on that index, not on any single book: every settlement-relevant quantity (strike distance, probabilities, provisional grading) uses the proxy; the line stays one exact book. The internal volatility series builds from the tape at 1-minute resolution, seeded from Kraken OHLC at load.

Feeds. Primary transport is a WebSocket stream per exchange with reconnect, backoff, and a 15 s silence watchdog; REST cross-verifies at 3 s (relaxed to ~12 s while a socket is healthy) and a REST print disputing its own socket by >25 bp flags drift. Settlement uses only prints fresh within 10 s.

Windows. Windows open on global quarter-hour time, phased 5-5-5. One strike per window, armable to T−5 s; arming over an existing strike replaces it and logs the prior as WITHDRAWN. Kalshi-loaded strikes settle the way Kalshi does — provisionally on the index proxy’s 60-second average at close, overwritten by the official result when it lands — while manually entered strikes settle by the multi-source vote. Calls tally against the 50% coin. Each settled window writes a window-analysis row. Logs persist in this browser.

Strike probabilities (calibrated, fit-2026-09-05-a). Calibrated against 2,000 settled KXBTC15M windows (Aug 15–Sep 5, 2026) with Kalshi’s own quote at eight points to expiry. Findings written into the engine: the market quote beats a lognormal at every horizon, so when a Kalshi quote exists the headline is a residual estimator — the quote as baseline, corrected by strike distance in realized-σ units and book spread, with frozen coefficients from a walk-forward fit (fees included; net edge in that history was indistinguishable from zero). Volatility is 60-minute realized (spine-matched to implied at short horizons), scaled by an intraday seasonal table (a 2× swing peaking 14–15 UTC) and a horizon term structure (+8–9% at 10–14 minutes, the market’s fat-tail premium). Without a quote the headline falls back to the median of analytic, drift and empirical; the flow estimator is a comparator only until recorded flow data grades it. Every estimator is scored separately in the edge ledger, one read per window at mid-window, net of Kalshi’s taker fee. Probabilities are clipped to [0.5%, 99.5%]. Nothing here is trading advice.

Signals. A microstructure read, recomputed each second, with every component labeled by what it can claim. Velocity is drift in bp/min over 60 s and 5 min with acceleration — descriptive; at these horizons momentum barely predicts. Regime is the AR(1) coefficient of 15-second returns over ten minutes, and its magnitude is shown as the directional read’s predictive weight, because that is what it is. Order-flow imbalance is net aggressor buy minus sell volume from the side-bearing trade streams — the legitimate form of “polling transactions” (price forms on exchange books; the chain settles ownership afterward, and transaction hashes carry no information), and the best-documented short-horizon predictor: real, signed, small. Dislocation compares the leader book to the peer cluster — an isolated gap is liquidity-driven and tends to revert under arbitrage (the honest form of an “artificial trough”); a cluster-wide move is information and tends to persist. Reaction levels map round numbers, hour open, 60-minute extremes and the strike by proximity — documented order clustering makes them zones, never calls. A fourth probability estimator, flow, tilts drift by imbalance and pulls the start price toward the peer cluster under isolated dislocation; the headline is the median of all four.

Tape extrapolation. The signals read is extended five minutes ahead at one-minute steps on its own stage. Velocity carries forward with persistence set by the regime coefficient (positive φ persists, compounded per minute; non-positive φ contributes nothing — no momentum is extrapolated into a random walk), order-flow tilt decays on a ~2-minute half-life, mean-reversion toward the 5-minute tape mean applies when the regime says so, and an isolated dislocation pulls toward the peer cluster. Every step is clamped inside 2.5σ√k and carried with its P5–P95 band. The path is pinned each minute and graded at each step as the tape arrives — error, band coverage, direction — against the flat do-nothing path, which is the only test that matters for a read: it earns its place where its error runs below flat, and the ledger says where.

Automatic behaviour. The projection band sets its own width from realized coverage (conformal: if 90% bands cover 84%, z widens until they cover 90%, and narrows when they over-cover); axis dynamics dilate instantly on jumps, contract slowly when calm, and lean into the price–strike duel from mid-window; the suggestion threshold is 1 point net of Kalshi’s taker fee. The Advanced section on DATA exposes these as overrides that apply only while a slider is moved off default; RESET restores automatic. Confidence is built by calibration, never by presentation — dollar gridlines are always true.

Kalshi. The KXBTC15M window is polled from Kalshi’s public API — the market record every 15 s (its floor_strike is the third-party strike, TBD until the window opens), the orderbook every 3 s, and the day’s ladder every 5 min. At each open the strike auto-loads into the arming tray (ABOVE = YES, BELOW = NO) and the quoted ¢ tracks the best ask on your chosen side, so the edge readout runs live; ARM remains yours. Kalshi settles on a 60-second average of the CF Benchmarks real-time index while this instrument votes six books at the instant, so the two can disagree at the margin — settled windows attach their official result beside our outcome in the rounds log with a ✓/✗ when the strike matched. Kalshi’s edge allows browser requests only from kalshi.com and localhost origins, so the page needs a path. Preferred — zero configuration: this repository carries a Pages Function at functions/api/[[path]].js; host the same repo on Cloudflare Pages (TUNE → CLOUDFLARE PAGES SETUP → Connect to Git → this repo → no build command, output directory / → Deploy) and the page finds /api on its own origin automatically — no URL to paste, and Cloudflare redeploys on every push. Alternatives: a Worker relay URL pasted into TUNE, or serving the file from localhost (python3 -m http.server 8080). TEST probes whichever path is active.

Viability. The backtest that replaced the order map: on 2,000 fifteen-minute windows and 240 hourly rungs, crossing the spread lost 4.2¢ per round trip and even a perfectly timed exit only broke even; posting as a maker captured 0.92¢ of spread against 1.68¢ of adverse selection and 0.87¢ of fees on the 15-minute series (1.03 / 2.34 / 0.87 hourly). No spread bucket, horizon, stop-loss or series inverted the sign. The panel carries those history rows and, beside them, this browser’s live measurement of the same three quantities — a resting bid at the best bid is deemed filled when the bid moves through it within a minute, adverse selection is the mid move in the minute after, fees are maker in and out — split further by the order-flow sign at posting time, the one filter history could not test. It reads NEGATIVE and turns positive only on net > 0 over at least thirty live fills. Nothing executes.

Swing watch. The one shape of intra-window trade the history leaves standing: buy a side priced under 15¢ and sell at 35¢ before the gate, never holding to settlement. Its economics are convex and unforgiving — at 10¢ in and 35¢ out the breakeven hit rate is 29% (31% with fees) against a history base rate of 12% for that price, so selection has to roughly triple the base rate. The tool prices the trade honestly every second: P(touch) is a first-passage estimate under the calibrated volatility — 600 simulated paths, the digital repriced each minute so the barrier moves as theta bites — beside the base rate, the breakeven and the net EV. Every cheap side is read once a minute and graded at the gate on the best bid seen after the read; the ledger scores the model against the base rate and splits hits by order-flow sign, the filter history could not test. The sweep tints a window amber only when the model clears breakeven and the ledger holds thirty graded reads where it beats the base rate. Nothing executes.

Simulation journal. The tool trades its own reads on paper, with money at stake and controls beside it. Ten arms — five entry rules by two exit rules — each start with a $1,000 bankroll and risk 5% of it at the ask on every trade, compounding. The universe is every side of the live window read at or under 15¢; the tool and model arms only enter at or under 9¢, where the convexity is. Box sells at 35¢ or at the last bid before the gate — the baseline. Trail is the trader: it arms a trailing exit once the bid reaches 2.5× entry, sells on a 25% pull-back from the peak, stops out at half the entry, and time-stops at T−1:30 if the position is under 2×. Fees on both legs. Each arm reports bankroll, maximum drawdown, trades, win rate, the 35¢ hit rate, mean P&L per share with a bootstrap interval, and a tally of exit reasons. The reads carried information only if a tool arm’s bankroll beats the all and random controls’ over at least thirty trades.

Settlement sources. Kalshi meters its history endpoints per IP, and the Cloudflare relay’s egress is shared with other tenants, so official results can arrive late. Every tracked window is therefore graded provisionally at close from the tape — the same 60-second average at close that Kalshi settles on, against the strike — or, for windows that predate the live tape, from the seeded 1-minute close (marked seed) — and overwritten when the official record lands; the ledger reports how often tape and official agreed, which doubles as a validation of the tape against the CF Benchmarks index. The CSV marks each window’s result source. A relay on a different provider’s IP pool can be pasted into TUNE if official timeliness matters.

Verdict. A pre-registered decision rule, computed live from the ledgers and written before the data arrived: at least 200 graded windows; Δ Brier (market minus model) positive with a 90% percentile-bootstrap interval excluding zero; at least one edge band with n ≥ 30 where the realized win rate beats the market-implied rate by ≥ 3 points; paper P&L positive over ≥ 100 entries and positive on each of the last two days separately. READY means every criterion passes. It is necessary, not sufficient, and it is the only condition under which this tool’s numbers should be trusted with size.

Edge ledger. With Kalshi connected, every phase window and every near-money hourly rung is snapshotted each minute — model probabilities from every estimator beside the market’s mid and both asks — and graded at settlement, one read per window at mid-window. Skill (Brier, log-loss) is reported per estimator against the market, and separately for 15-minute windows and hourly rungs. Edge bands take one paper position per window at the ask, net of fee. Calibration deciles compare stated and realized rates. All ledgers persist and export to CSV; the verdict rule reads 15-minute windows only.

Chart. One perpetual sweep on an onyx field: thirty minutes behind the cursor, fifteen ahead, never wiping. Kalshi’s quarter-hour gates arrive from the right and leave to the left, the hour gate heavier; each window carries its strike in gilt across its own span, and a settled window collapses to a cabochon at its gate. The line is the leader book, constituent-verified: malachite while the call is winning or the price rising, ruby while it is losing or falling. The strike stays in view and the domain centres on the span it shares with the recent tape, leaning into the duel through the final minutes. When the tool arm opens a swing on the live window, that window inverts — saturated mineral field, bone tape, full-gilt strike, every other window dimmed to onyx. SWING locks the view tight on that window; dragging a rectangle on the field locks any region; RESUME or Escape releases. The light on every slab, bevel and cabochon follows the sun over Dayton (or your location, from Advanced), computed here every minute — the same sundial as the rhyme instrument.
