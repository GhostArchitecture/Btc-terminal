Aggregation. Spot = median of sources fresher than 20 s. Sources polled every 5 s (CoinGecko 60 s; it is itself a composite index). Binance.com excluded: HTTP 451 geo-block for US clients.

Seeding. Return statistics are initialized from Kraken 1-minute OHLC (last ~180 bars), then rolled forward from live 1-minute bars of the median.

Models. Volatility: EWMA (RiskMetrics, λ = 0.94) on 1-min log returns. Martingale: E[P] = spot; 90% band = ±1.645·σ√h. GBM: 1,500 Monte-Carlo paths, drift = mean of last 120 one-min returns; cone = per-step percentiles. OU: AR(1) on log-price deviation from a 30-min mean; deviation decays as bʰ; suppressed when no reversion is detected (b ≥ 0.999). Momentum: EWMA drift (10-min half-life) extrapolated, clamped to ±2.5·σ√h. Ensemble: median of available point forecasts.

Chart. Two states. Armed or live, the frame is the round: −5 minutes of context, the window phased 5-5-5 with clock-mapped majors and minute minors, price gridlines snapped to round dollar steps that rescale with the zoom. The plot is a single designated feed — Coinbase primary, failing over down the source order if it stales — color-keyed to the strike (green above, red below), with the active feed named on the frame. Multi-source verification lives where it adjudicates: per-source dots at the open and expiry marks, the live side-count and spread, and the settlement vote. Idle, the frame is a rolling 15-minute trend-colored view with the entry window's state and countdown named. The projection engine runs unseen and reports to this page.

Feeds. Primary transport is a WebSocket stream per exchange — sub-second prints, throttled to ~0.4 s history density — with automatic reconnect and backoff and a 15 s silence watchdog. REST polling continues as cross-verification: full 3 s cadence when a socket is down, relaxed to ~12 s while it is healthy; a REST print disputing its own socket by more than 25 bp flags the source as drift rather than overwriting it. No socket, no problem — the source degrades to REST transparently, and settlement votes only ever use prints fresh within 10 s regardless of transport.

Strike probabilities. While a round is live, three classical estimators read the chance the settlement lands above the armed strike, recomputed each second as time-to-expiry decays: the analytic lognormal (the Black-Scholes digital probability under the EWMA volatility, martingale drift), a drift-adjusted variant tilted by the capped momentum estimate, and an empirical read — the historical frequency of τ-minute moves exceeding the required distance, taken from the recorded bar series with no distributional assumption (overlapping samples; effective n is smaller than it looks). The headline OVER and UNDER figures are the median of the three; the per-model breakdown reports below the rounds table. These are distribution reads under stated assumptions, not signals: drift moves the number by a few hundredths at these horizons, and saturation toward 0/100 near expiry with price pinned is the correct shape, not confidence. The final pre-settle read is logged per round and colored by whether it sided with the outcome — the probability engine is audited like everything else.

Rounds. A round is a 15-minute window on quarter-hour wall-clock boundaries, phased 5-5-5. Because the strike is third-party information revealed only after the interval opens, entry happens inside a 90-second buffer beginning at the boundary: arm within it and the round attaches to the running window; miss it and the window is gated — no late entry, no exceptions. The open reference is reconstructed from the recorded per-source prints at the exact boundary instant (the feeds stream regardless of arming), so a late arm never shifts the mark; settlement is pinned to the quarter-hour sharp regardless of entry time. Disarm is possible only while the buffer remains. At expiry each feed fresh within 10 s votes above or below the strike: ≥⅔ settles UP or DOWN, a split settles DISPUTED, under two voters settles VOID. Calls tally against the 50% coin; the round log is session-scoped.

Interval discipline. Every minute one forecast set is pinned to five wall-clock due times. Realized price at each due instant is linearly interpolated from the tick series (never a late sample; residual resolution lag is reported in the scorecard header). If no tick exists within 45 s of a due instant — a suspended tab, a feed outage — that resolution is voided, not scored. Accuracy is scored per interval — the five horizons are never blended. Sets are spaced 60 s apart so consecutive resolutions are not pseudo-replicated autocorrelated samples; the matrix above refreshes faster (≤20 s) for display, but accountability runs on the pinned sets.

Benchmark. The martingale MAE equals the realized move magnitude — the floor every other model must beat. At 5–60 min horizons BTC log-price is empirically near-martingale: expected drift is ~1–3 bp against a typical σ of 30–100 bp, so point forecasts cluster at spot and direction hit rates oscillate around 50%. The informative output is the dispersion (cone), not the direction. Nothing here is trading advice; the scorecard exists to measure these models against live data rather than assert their skill.
