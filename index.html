Aggregation. Spot = median of sources fresher than 20 s. Sources polled every 5 s (CoinGecko 60 s; it is itself a composite index). Binance.com excluded: HTTP 451 geo-block for US clients.

Seeding. Return statistics are initialized from Kraken 1-minute OHLC (last ~180 bars), then rolled forward from live 1-minute bars of the median.

Models. Volatility: EWMA (RiskMetrics, λ = 0.94) on 1-min log returns. Martingale: E[P] = spot; 90% band = ±1.645·σ√h. GBM: 1,500 Monte-Carlo paths, drift = mean of last 120 one-min returns; cone = per-step percentiles. OU: AR(1) on log-price deviation from a 30-min mean; deviation decays as bʰ; suppressed when no reversion is detected (b ≥ 0.999). Momentum: EWMA drift (10-min half-life) extrapolated, clamped to ±2.5·σ√h. Ensemble: median of available point forecasts.

Rounds. A round is a 15-minute window aligned to quarter-hour wall-clock boundaries, subdivided by 3-minute marks. Arming requires a USD strike (and a call, above/below) entered before the boundary — arming inside the final 5 s rolls to the following window, and no entry can join a window already running. Armed rounds lock 5 s before open and cannot be edited after; one round may be staged while another runs. At open, fresh per-source prints are recorded (open marker = midrange of those prints, a marker only). At expiry each exchange feed fresh within 10 s votes above or below the strike: ≥⅔ on one side settles UP or DOWN, a split settles DISPUTED (strike inside the inter-source spread), fewer than two voters settles VOID. Calls tally against the 50% coin. The round frame plots one line per exchange — no aggregate; agreement is the braid's tightness, and the live spread is printed. The statistical engine elsewhere retains its internal consensus series, which models require. Exchange feeds poll every 3 s; the round log is session-scoped.

Interval discipline. Every minute one forecast set is pinned to five wall-clock due times. Realized price at each due instant is linearly interpolated from the tick series (never a late sample; residual resolution lag is reported in the scorecard header). If no tick exists within 45 s of a due instant — a suspended tab, a feed outage — that resolution is voided, not scored. Accuracy is scored per interval — the five horizons are never blended. Sets are spaced 60 s apart so consecutive resolutions are not pseudo-replicated autocorrelated samples; the matrix above refreshes faster (≤20 s) for display, but accountability runs on the pinned sets.

Chart. The frame originates ~30 minutes before projection onset and runs to +60: context on the left of the ONSET line, the pinned period on the right, x-marks at +5/10/15/30/60 labeled with actual clock times, cursor sweeping left to right. Two lines duel: the control — actual consensus price at tick resolution — against the pinned projection median. The area between them over the elapsed region is tinted by sign (green where actual runs above projection, red below), so divergence is read spatially; a live Δ readout in bp rides the head. Marks resolve in place (ring at the projection, dot at the print, green inside that set's 90% band); at the hour the five results commit to the period log, the frame wipes, and clock labels remap. A tab suspended past a boundary re-anchors at wake rather than fabricating a retroactive set. The y-axis holds current price at center and zooms adaptively — tightening while the control tracks the band, widening on excursions; the zoom is presentation only and the dollar gridlines are always true. Band coverage against its 90% target remains the calibration verdict; point-forecast confidence is martingale-capped, and dispersion is the claim under audit.

Benchmark. The martingale MAE equals the realized move magnitude — the floor every other model must beat. At 5–60 min horizons BTC log-price is empirically near-martingale: expected drift is ~1–3 bp against a typical σ of 30–100 bp, so point forecasts cluster at spot and direction hit rates oscillate around 50%. The informative output is the dispersion (cone), not the direction. Nothing here is trading advice; the scorecard exists to measure these models against live data rather than assert their skill.
